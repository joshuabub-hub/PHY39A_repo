## Part 5.2: PWM and the slope-ratio model

For a PWM period $\tau$, let current of magnitude $I$ flow during the
on-time $D\tau$ and be zero during the remaining $(1-D)\tau$. Then

$$
\langle I\rangle = \frac{1}{\tau}\int_0^\tau I(t)\,dt
= \frac{I(D\tau)}{\tau}=DI,
\qquad
\langle I^2\rangle = \frac{1}{\tau}\int_0^\tau I(t)^2\,dt
= \frac{I^2(D\tau)}{\tau}=DI^2.
$$

Thus the average resistive/Joule heating, proportional to $I^2R$, also
scales with duty cycle $D$ (under the ideal on/off-current approximation).

Define signed duty cycle $d=u/255$ and $D=\lvert d\rvert$. Let $\dot{Q}_P>0$ be
the full-on Peltier heat rate into the object and $\dot{Q}_J>0$ the
full-on Joule heat rate deposited at the object face. Use

$$
\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{TEC}}=d\dot{Q}_P+\lvert d\rvert\dot{Q}_J,
\qquad
C\frac{dT}{dt}=\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{TEC}}-G(T-T_0).
$$

At steady state, $dT/dt=0$ and $T=T_0+\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{TEC}}/G$.
For heating, $d\geq0$ and $\lvert d\rvert=d$, so
$\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{TEC}}=d(\dot{Q}_P+\dot{Q}_J)$. For cooling, $d<0$ and
$\lvert d\rvert=-d$, so $\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{TEC}}=d(\dot{Q}_P-\dot{Q}_J)$. Differentiating
the steady-state temperatures with respect to signed duty cycle gives

$$
m_h=\frac{dT_h}{dd}=\frac{\dot{Q}_P+\dot{Q}_J}{G},
\qquad
m_c=\frac{dT_c}{dd}=\frac{\dot{Q}_P-\dot{Q}_J}{G}.
$$

When $\dot{Q}_P>\dot{Q}_J$, both branch derivatives are positive and
$m_h>m_c$. The slopes per signed PWM count each include the same factor
$1/255$, which cancels in the ratio $r=m_h/m_c$. Therefore,

$$
r=\frac{\dot{Q}_P+\dot{Q}_J}{\dot{Q}_P-\dot{Q}_J}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\dot{Q}_J}{\dot{Q}_P}=\frac{r-1}{r+1}.
$$

With the full-range measured value $r=3.76$,

$$
\frac{\dot{Q}_J}{\dot{Q}_P}
=\frac{3.76-1}{3.76+1}\approx 0.580.
$$

The measured slope ratio therefore implies an object-face full-on Joule
heat rate about **0.58 times** the full-on Peltier heat rate, under this
model. As a check, if $r=2$, the formula gives $\dot{Q}_J/\dot{Q}_P=1/3$.

## Part 5.3: Laird data-sheet calculation

The specified module is the Laird Thermal Systems **CP14-127-045-L2-W4.5**
(manufacturer part number **58910-501**). Values below are from the
“SPECIFICATIONS” table on page 3 of the
[Laird datasheet](https://sethfraden.github.io/Phys39F26-course/references/laird-tec-cp14-127-045.pdf),
using its $T_{\mathrm{hot}}=27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ column.

| Datasheet parameter | Value | Meaning and stated condition |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| $R_M$ (module resistance) | $1.50\,\Omega$ | Electrical module resistance; datasheet column $T_{\mathrm{hot}}=27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. |
| $I_{\max}$ | $8.6\,\mathrm{A}$ | Listed as current at $\Delta T_{\max}$; $T_{\mathrm{hot}}=27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. |
| $Q_{c,\max}$ | $71.3\,\mathrm{W}$ | Maximum heat pumped at the cold side, at $\Delta T=0$; $T_{\mathrm{hot}}=27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. |
| $\Delta T_{\max}$ | $70.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ | Maximum hot-to-cold temperature difference at $Q_c=0$; $T_{\mathrm{hot}}=27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. |

For the usual approximately equal split of Joule heat between the module's
two faces, the object's-face Joule rate at the listed current is

$$
\dot{Q}_{J,\max}=\frac{1}{2}I_{\max}^2R_M
=\frac{1}{2}(8.6\,\mathrm{A})^2(1.50\,\Omega)
=55.47\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

At $\Delta T=0$, the cold-face heat pumped is the Peltier contribution
minus the cold-face share of Joule heating. Thus, using the datasheet
maximum-current values in the requested lumped model,

$$
\dot{Q}_{P,\max}\approx Q_{c,\max}+\dot{Q}_{J,\max}
=71.3+55.47=126.77\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

This gives the datasheet-based maximum-current slope-ratio prediction

$$
r_{\mathrm{Laird,max}}
=\frac{\dot{Q}_{P,\max}+\dot{Q}_{J,\max}}
{\dot{Q}_{P,\max}-\dot{Q}_{J,\max}}
=\frac{126.77+55.47}{126.77-55.47}
\approx 2.56.
$$

The calculation is an estimate using datasheet headline values: the table
defines $I_{\max}$ at $\Delta T_{\max}$, while $Q_{c,\max}$ is specified
at $\Delta T=0$. These extrema are not explicitly guaranteed to be a
single simultaneous operating point, so the resulting ratio should not be
treated as a precision prediction.

## Part 5.4: Compare the ratios

The measured full-range ratio is $r\approx3.76$, versus the
datasheet-based estimate $r_{\mathrm{Laird,max}}\approx2.56$. The measured value
is about $47\%$ higher. Exact agreement is not expected: the datasheet
estimate combines its rated maximum-current and maximum-capacity values,
whereas the experiment uses a different heatsink, contact, ambient
temperature, and operating range. Module resistance and thermoelectric
coefficients also vary with temperature, and the H-bridge/supply may
change the current from the ideal model.

In particular, a 100% PWM command ($D=1$) only means the controller holds
the output on continuously. It does **not** regulate the TEC to
$I_{\max}$: actual current is set by supply voltage, module resistance,
driver voltage drop, wiring, and thermal operating conditions. Conversely,
the Laird $I_{\max}$ is a datasheet rating defined at its specified test
conditions, not a current guaranteed by this rig at PWM 255.

### Passive heat conduction (also Part 5.4)

The passive heat-flow term into the object is $-G(T-T_0)$. If the object is
hotter than the room ($T>T_0$), passive heat flows from the object to the
room. If the object is colder ($T<T_0$), heat flows from the room into the
object. This approximately symmetric conduction opposes either deviation
from ambient: it removes heat in the heating branch and adds heat in the
cooling branch. In the linear model it contributes the same conductance
$G$ to both branch slopes, so it cannot by itself explain unequal slope
magnitudes; the difference comes from the branch-dependent Peltier and
Joule terms.
